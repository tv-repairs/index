Scope of Practice

Upholding clear professional boundaries, cultural humility, and interdisciplinary coordination to support your whole-person healing journey.

Positionality & Coaching Philosophy

My name is Comrade Tom (he/they). I serve as an affirming, non-judgmental thinking partner for individuals navigating the tension between the life they have and the life they want.

As a student of Integrative Health and Wellbeing Coaching at the University of Minnesota’s Bakken Center for Spirituality & Healing and practicing under the MindBody coaching guidelines, my approach centers on your unique bodily wisdom and strengths. Operating from a place of unconditional positive regard, my goal is to help you clear the static, adjust the dial, and build steady habit architectures that align your day-to-day actions with your core values.

Defining Our Scope: A scope of practice defines the professional activities a practitioner is educated, trained, competent, and legally authorized to provide. It reflects my knowledge, skills, credentials, and demonstrated competencies. Adhering strictly to these boundaries helps protect both you and my practice by promoting safe, ethical, culturally responsive, and legally compliant services.

MindBody coaching works collaboratively in a client-centered process designed to support self-awareness, personal growth, resilience, behavior change, and the achievement of self-determined goals. In our sessions, I incorporate embodied, mindfulness-based, relational, and strengths-based approaches to support your learning, development, and somatic self-efficacy.

"Coaching is distinct from psychotherapy, counseling, medical care, or other licensed health professions. I do not diagnose mental health or medical conditions, prescribe treatment, or provide psychotherapy or other regulated clinical interventions."

— Comrade Tom

A Commitment to Equity: As a 39-year-old, cisgender, queer, white man, my practice is shaped by the recognition of my visible privileges. I strive to practice cultural humility, operating as an active co-conspirator for equity. Guided by universal design principles and a desire to reduce socioeconomic barriers, I utilize a tiered, community-supported pricing structure to ensure integrative wellness is accessible to those who need it most.

Understanding My Scope

Clarifying the boundaries between client-centered MindBody Coaching and clinical psychotherapy.

Coaching Scope (Within Practice)

Collaboratively supporting self-awareness, resilience, behavior change, and self-determined goals:

  • Embodied & Relational Integration: Incorporating embodied, mindfulness-based, relational, and strengths-based approaches to support learning and development.
  • Personal Resource Access: Helping clients clarify goals, access personal resources, increase self-awareness, and develop actionable strategies.
  • Grounded Habits: Designing gentle, customized accountability structures to sustain your chosen lifestyle changes without burnout.
  • Pattern Inquiry: Using the Enneagram and Mind-Body skills to identify unconscious default patterns and somatic reactions.
  • Interdisciplinary Alignment: Collaborating with your clinical therapist or physician (with written consent) to align our coaching actions with your clinical milestones.
  • Safe Referrals: Recommending and providing immediate, appropriate referrals to qualified professionals when client needs fall outside my competence.

Clinical Scope (Outside Practice)

Strict boundaries referred to licensed medical, psychiatric, or psychological professionals:

  • No Clinical Diagnostics: Identifying, assessing, or diagnosing physical, mental, or psychological health conditions.
  • No Clinical Psychotherapy: Providing psychotherapy, clinical counseling, or other regulated clinical interventions.
  • No Medical Prescriptions: Recommending, prescribing, or adjusting medications, clinical treatments, or specific dietary/exercise plans.
  • No Trauma Reprocessing: Direct reprocessing of core clinical trauma or developmental trauma wounds (which remains the domain of licensed therapists).
  • No Expert Directing: Giving unsolicited advice, telling you what to do, or imposing goals from a position of expertise.

Interdisciplinary Care Coordination

Integrative healing thrives when your support network is in sync. For clients currently working with a licensed mental health professional, I am eager to establish a collaborative line of communication (strictly with your written consent). This ensures that our coaching habit-architecture is aligned with and actively supports your clinical therapeutic goals.
